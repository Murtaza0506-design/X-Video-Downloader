# The Handover

A novel. Leeds and the Pennines, from eleven days before the collapse to two
years after it.

The structure below is the brief, mapped to chapters. Every chapter is meant to
do at least two of: change the situation, reveal character, deepen a
relationship, introduce a new problem.

## The revelation (chapter 16), and why it is placed there

It is not the bite. Everybody is already carrying it and has been since before
anyone noticed. Dying is the trigger, by any means at all.

This is put at chapter 16 so that it lands *after* chapter 11, in which the
group makes an appalling decision about Wendy on the assumption that a bite is
a death sentence. The revelation does not open a door. It closes one, behind
them, on something they cannot now undo. Chapter 18 is the marriage of those
two chapters.

## People

- **Neil Cowie**, 31. Assistant category manager, ambient snacks. Unremarkable
  and content to be. Fails to do the necessary thing in chapter 5 and spends
  the rest of the book paying for it.
- **Priti Sandhu**, 44. PE teacher. Becomes the one who decides, and hates it.
- **Callum Hutchinson (Hutch)**, 17. Says everything twice: once for the record
  and once for himself.
- **Erin Mowat**, 34. Locum pharmacist. Holds the drug bag, so she holds the
  decisions nobody else wants.
- **Dermot Feeley**, 46. Regional sales. Likeable, plausible, and the reason
  they lose Hillfoot.
- **Wendy Ashraf**, 38, and **Nadia**, 7. The mother and the child. Chapter 11
  is about them.
- **Jo Cowie**, 27. Neil's sister. Two streets away.
- **Sue Ellerby**, 61. Thirty years in the same building. Never meets any of
  them. Chapter 24 is hers.

## Terms

- **the Handover** — the Executive's word for the collapse. Everyone uses it.
- **the Executive** — National Recovery Executive, seated at Harrogate.
- **non-viable / nivs** — the official word, and the word people actually use.
- **Adjustment** — Post-Event Adjustment Disorder. Universal, therefore a joke.
