### Description

Paste this into the Description box exactly as it is. KDP accepts these tags
and it comes to about 1,900 characters of the 4,000 allowed.

```html
<h4>Most quotation books hand you a beautiful sentence and walk away.</h4>
<p>You read forty in a sitting, feel briefly elevated, and by Thursday you could not name one of them.</p>
<p>This one is built the other way round. Every line arrives with two things: <b>what it actually means</b>, in plain English, and <b>one specific place to put it</b>. The row you are rehearsing in the car. The email you have not answered. The thing you keep not starting.</p>
<p>Three hundred and fifteen entries, arranged by the problem you have rather than the century it came from. Marcus Aurelius on obstacles. Aesop on persuasion. An English proverb on knowing when to leave a thing alone. They contradict each other in places, which is what real advice does.</p>
<h4>Twenty-one chapters, arranged by what is actually bothering you</h4>
<p>Starting before you feel ready · When things go wrong · Anger · Worry · Wanting less · Envy and comparison · Friendship and love · Listening properly · Saying the hard thing · When someone wrongs you · Persuading without pushing · Being wrong in public · Being alone · Getting started · Keeping going · Knowing when to stop · Rest and doing nothing · Money · Time and where it goes · Getting older · What actually matters</p>
<h4>What makes it different</h4>
<ul>
<li><b>Every line is explained.</b> Not admired. Explained, in a paragraph, assuming no interest in the century it came from.</li>
<li><b>Every line has a use.</b> Not "be more patient" but a situation you will recognise from this week.</li>
<li><b>It disagrees with itself.</b> One entry tells you to persist, another to stop. Deciding which applies today is the part that is yours.</li>
<li><b>It gives you permission to stop.</b> Most chapters end with an entry that lets you off: leave the grievance unforgiven, skip the difficult conversation, put the book down.</li>
<li><b>The attributions are honest.</b> A note at the back explains why so many famous quotations belong to nobody in particular, and every disputed line in the book is marked.</li>
</ul>
<p>151 sources, from Seneca to a nursery rhyme about an owl. A full index of them at the back, every entry a link.</p>
<p><i>Open it anywhere.</i></p>
```

### Preview before you save

Open the online previewer and check three things:

1. The cover is the first page.
2. The menu's table of contents lists all 21 chapters under four parts.
3. Tap a number in the Index of Sources. It should jump to that entry.

## Paperback

Use *Create Paperback* from the finished Kindle title so the two link.

| KDP field | What to enter |
|---|---|
| **ISBN** | *Get a free KDP ISBN*. It cannot be used with any other printer, which is the only cost |
| **Publication date** | leave empty, so it uses the live date |
| **Print options** | Black & white interior with **cream** paper |
| **Trim size** | **5.5 × 8.5 in** |
| **Bleed** | **No bleed** |
| **Cover finish** | **Matte** |
| **Manuscript** | `interior.pdf` |
| **Cover** | `cover-cream.pdf` — again, not Cover Creator |
| List price | **£9.99** / **$12.99**. Check the printing cost KDP shows and keep the royalty above about £2 |

If you pick white paper instead, upload `cover-white.pdf`. The two differ only
in spine width, because the papers are different thicknesses, and the wrong one
will be rejected or will print with the spine off centre.

### Expect one warning

The paperback previewer will flag that the interior has no bleed. That is
correct and intentional: nothing in the book runs to the edge of the page, so
it is supplied at trim size. Accept it and continue.

## After it is live

- Both editions appear on one product page, with a Kindle/Paperback selector,
  usually within 72 hours.
- Set up an **Author Central** page at author.amazon.com and claim the book.
  It puts a photograph and a biography on the listing and takes ten minutes.
- The first review matters more than the next twenty. Ask three people who
  will actually read it.
