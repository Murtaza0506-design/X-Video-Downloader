# Chapter 8 — Ending It

An ending letter has one job that people get wrong: it has to end the thing. A great many of them are written to be kind and end up being ambiguous, and ambiguity is the cruellest available option, because it leaves the other person doing the work of interpretation for weeks.

The kindness is real. It belongs elsewhere in the letter. Put the ending in the first two sentences, in the indicative, with no conditionals and no *I think* attached. Then be as warm as you truthfully can be about everything else. That order feels backwards while writing and is the right way round for reading.

The other error is the reason. Endings tempt people into a full account of everything that went wrong, on the theory that the other person is owed an explanation. Sometimes they are. But a comprehensive list of failings is not an explanation, it is a prosecution, and it is being delivered at the moment the person can do nothing about any of it. One honest reason is a kindness. Nine is an assault.

Do not ask them to understand. Do not ask them to agree it is for the best. And do not leave a door ajar you have no intention of opening, however much better it makes the paragraph feel.

> "You are resigning." — The resignation

**The letter.** Dear Ruth,||I am writing to resign from my role as Operations Manager. My notice is three months, so my last day would be the fourteenth of June.||I have taken a position at the Trust. It is a step up in scope and it was not something I went looking for.||I want to say properly that these four years have been the best of my working life, and that most of what I can do now I learned here, a good deal of it from you.||I would like to leave this in a state where nobody is picking up loose ends. I will put together a handover plan this week and I am happy to be flexible about the date if a different one works better for the migration.||Thank you, genuinely.||Murtaza

**What is doing the work.** The date in the first paragraph. A resignation that buries the leaving date makes the reader hunt for the only fact that matters. The warmth in the third paragraph is unqualified and therefore believable, and the handover offer is the thing that determines what people say about you for the next ten years, which is the actual purpose of a resignation letter.

> "You are ending a business relationship with a client." — Resigning the account

**The letter.** Dear Sarah,||After a good deal of thought, we are going to step back from the account at the end of the current agreement on the thirtieth of April.||This is not about any single thing and it is not about money. It is that the work has moved a long way from what we are good at, and I would rather say so than keep doing it adequately.||Everything through to the thirtieth will be completed as agreed. I will also put together a full handover pack, and I am happy to speak to whoever picks it up.||I can recommend two firms who would be a better fit than us, if that would be useful.||It has been four years and I am grateful for all of them.||Murtaza

**What is doing the work.** Declining to itemise. *Not about any single thing* pre-empts the reasonable question and refuses the row, and the honest reason offered instead is about fit rather than fault, which is both true and impossible to argue with. The recommendation of successors is what converts an ending into something the client remembers well.

> "A friendship has become one-sided and you want to stop." — Letting a friendship go

**The letter.** Dear Chris,||I have been putting this off for a while.||I do not think I can carry on with the friendship as it is. For a long time now the contact has been in one direction, and the times we do speak are mostly about your situation. I have not minded that for stretches; I do mind that when things were bad for me last year you did not notice, and I have realised I stopped telling you.||I am not looking for an argument about whether that is fair and I am not asking you to fix it.||I am not angry with you. I am just tired, and I would rather step back honestly than keep drifting and pretending I am busy.||I hope things get easier for you. I mean that.||Ade

**What is doing the work.** One specific instance, not a general characterisation. *You are selfish* is arguable; *when things were bad for me you did not notice* is a fact and cannot be debated away. Declining the argument in advance is the part that makes it survivable for both of you, and the refusal to pretend to be busy is the honesty the letter turns on.

> "You need distance from a family member." — Asking for space, without a rupture

**The letter.** Dad,||I am going to take a step back from contact for a while. I do not know how long and I am not going to pretend I do.||This is not about a single argument and there is nothing you can do this week to change it. It is that I come away from most of our conversations feeling smaller than when I went in, and I have stopped being able to absorb that.||I am not cutting you off and I am not asking anybody else in the family to take a side. I will be at Grandma's in June.||I will get in touch. Please do not ring in the meantime; it would make this harder rather than shorter.||Murtaza

**What is doing the work.** Setting the terms out loud, including the one nobody remembers to set: what happens next, and who initiates it. Without that line the distance becomes a negotiation conducted through voicemail. Naming an event he will still attend prevents the whole family reading it as a rupture, which is what escalates these.

> "You are ending a relationship." — The letter you should probably not send

**The letter.** Rachel,||I am ending our relationship. I am sorry, and I know that reading it in writing is not what you deserve.||I have tried to say this to you three times and each time I have not managed it, and a fourth failed attempt seemed worse than this.||The reason is not a single thing you did. I have known for months that I am not in it in the way you are, and continuing while knowing that is the thing I am most ashamed of.||I will move my things out on Saturday while you are at your mother's, if that is easiest. I will pay the rent through to the end of the quarter.||If you want to talk, I will talk, whenever and for as long as you want.||I am sorry, Rachel.||Ade

**What is doing the work.** Admitting that the medium is wrong, in the second sentence, and giving the true reason for it. This letter is here as the exception that proves the chapter: almost every ending of this size should happen face to face, and the only defensible version in writing is one that says so and gives the true reason instead of dressing the choice up as consideration.
