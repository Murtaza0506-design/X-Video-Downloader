# Chapter 10 — Money With Institutions

Writing to an organisation is a different craft. Nobody is reading it in the way you imagine. Your letter goes to somebody with a queue, a script and no great authority, and the outcome depends on making their job easy rather than on making them care.

That reframing changes almost everything. Emotion is the strongest tool in every other chapter of this book. Here it is close to useless, and it works against you. The person reading has been shouted at four times today and has the defences to prove it. What works instead is being the easiest correspondent in the queue.

Which means: dates, reference numbers, amounts, and one specific request. Say what happened, when, with the reference. Say what you want, as a number or an action. Say what you will do next if it is not resolved, without threatening. Keep it to one screen. What you are aiming at is a message that somebody with eleven minutes, four other cases and a screen full of mandatory fields can act on without having to ask you a single question, because every question they have to ask costs you a week.

The other thing worth knowing is that persistence beats force. Most complaints are abandoned at the second reply, and organisations are structured around that fact, by design or not. The person who sends a courteous fourth message with a reference number attached is in a small minority, and is treated as one.

> "An invoice is six weeks overdue." — The freelancer's chase

**The letter.** Dear Accounts,||Invoice 2041, dated 3 January, for £2,400, is now 42 days overdue. Payment terms were 14 days.||Could you confirm the payment date? If there is a problem with the invoice, tell me what it is and I will correct it today.||For reference: the work was signed off by Sarah Miles on 20 December and the purchase order number is PO-88213.||If I have not had a payment date by Friday the 21st, I will invoke the Late Payment of Commercial Debts Act, which entitles me to statutory interest and a fixed recovery charge. I would much rather not and I do not expect it to be necessary.||With thanks,||Murtaza Raza

**What is doing the work.** Every fact the accounts department needs to act, in one place: invoice number, date, amount, days overdue, sign-off, purchase order. Most chasers omit at least two of these and the reply is a request for them, which costs another week. The statutory reference is stated as a step you will take rather than as a threat, and *I would much rather not* is what keeps it from souring.

> "Something you bought is faulty and you want your money back." — The complaint that works

**The letter.** Dear Sir or Madam,||Order 55-2093, placed 14 February. The dishwasher was delivered on the 22nd and has not completed a cycle without stopping.||Under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 I am within the thirty days for a full refund of £429, which is what I am requesting.||An engineer attended on 3 March, reference ENG-4471, and confirmed the fault is with the pump. I have the report.||Please confirm the refund and arrange collection. If it is easier for you to replace rather than refund, I am open to that provided it is delivered within seven days.||I look forward to hearing from you.||Murtaza Raza

**What is doing the work.** Naming the legislation and the specific right, which moves the letter out of the discretionary pile and into the compliance pile. Offering the replacement as an alternative is the part people leave out. Give the organisation a cheaper way to satisfy you and the odds of a fast answer go up. You lose nothing by allowing it.

> "You cannot pay a bill and it is going to get worse." — Telling a creditor before they chase

**The letter.** Dear Sir or Madam,||Account 40028811. I am writing before the next payment is due rather than after it, because I am not going to be able to make it.||My circumstances changed in January when I was made redundant. I have applied for benefits and I have a start date for new work in April.||What I am proposing: £40 a month for the next three months instead of £210, and a return to the full amount from May, with the arrears added at £30 a month from June.||I would rather agree something now than default. I can provide evidence of the redundancy and the new contract.||I am contactable on the number on the account any weekday.||Murtaza Raza

**What is doing the work.** Arriving first. Creditors treat a proactive proposal as a different kind of file from a missed payment, because it moves the file out of collections and into arrangements. The proposal is specific, includes a return to normal, and deals with the arrears. That last part is the one people omit, and it decides whether the offer is accepted.

> "There is a charge on your bill you did not agree to." — Disputing a charge

**The letter.** Dear Sir or Madam,||Account 7712-0043. The March bill includes a charge of £89.99 described as "Service Activation". I did not request this service and I have no record of agreeing to it.||I am disputing the charge and asking for it to be removed and refunded.||I have checked my correspondence back to December and there is no confirmation email of the kind you send when a service is added. If you hold a recording or a record of my consent, please send it to me.||I have paid the remainder of the bill in full so that the account is not in arrears while this is resolved.||Please confirm within fourteen days.||Murtaza Raza

**What is doing the work.** Paying the undisputed part. It removes their strongest lever, which is treating you as a defaulter. It also shows good faith, and that makes the dispute easier to concede. Asking them to produce the evidence of consent puts the burden where it belongs, and it is the sentence that ends it.

> "You have complained twice and been fobbed off twice." — Escalating properly

**The letter.** Dear Sir or Madam,||Complaint reference CMP-90211, first raised 4 February, now seven weeks old.||I am asking for a deadlock letter or a final response so that I can refer this to the Ombudsman.||The history: I reported the fault on 4 February. I was told on the 19th that an engineer would attend within five working days; none did. On 12 March I was told the case had been closed, which nobody had informed me of. It was reopened on my call the same day.||I am not asking for anything beyond the original request, which was the repair and a refund of the two months of service charges, £58.||If a final response is not issued within eight weeks of the original complaint I will refer the matter regardless, as I am entitled to do.||Murtaza Raza

**What is doing the work.** Asking for the deadlock letter. It signals that you know the regulatory process, and organisations behave differently towards a complainant who knows what it is. The dated chronology is the other half. The ombudsman will want it, and the company knows somebody outside the building will read it.
