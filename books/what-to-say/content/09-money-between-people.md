# Chapter 9 — Money Between People

Money between friends is difficult for a reason that has nothing to do with money. Every other favour between friends is settled by reciprocity over time, without accounting. Money introduces a number, and a number cannot be settled that way. Either it comes back or it does not. Both of you know which. That is the whole difficulty in a line: an arrangement that used to run on goodwill and rough symmetry now runs on a figure that either appears in an account by a date or fails to, and goodwill has no way of settling it.

That is why these letters are the hardest in the book to write and the most avoided. The lender feels grasping for asking. The borrower feels shamed by being asked. And the friendship absorbs the difference. Both parties see less of each other, neither says why, and that is the outcome nobody wanted and everybody chose.

The single most useful principle here is to separate the money from the relationship out loud. Say that the friendship is not conditional on the money, and mean it. Then deal with the money with a number and a date, because vagueness is what allows the thing to fester.

The second principle is about lending: if you cannot afford to give it, you cannot afford to lend it. Every letter in this chapter is easier if that rule was applied at the start. Most of them exist because it was not.

---

> "A friend borrowed money and has not mentioned it in four months." — Asking for it back

**The letter.** Chris,||This is awkward and I would rather be awkward than weird with you, so I am just going to say it.||Could I ask about the four hundred from October? I am not in trouble and there is no crisis, but it has been a while and I have noticed I have started thinking about it, which I do not want.||If it is difficult, tell me and we will do fifty a month, or whatever works. If you had genuinely forgotten, which is completely possible, then no problem at all.||It is not a big deal and it is not going to become one. I just did not want it sitting there unsaid.||Ade

**What is doing the work.** Naming the awkwardness, which drains most of it. Then the crucial admission: *I have started thinking about it, which I do not want*, which explains why the letter exists in terms of the friendship rather than the finances. Offering the instalment option before he has to ask removes the humiliation of having to propose it himself.

---

> "A friend has asked to borrow money and you do not want to lend it." — Refusing to lend

**The letter.** Sam,||I am sorry, I am not going to be able to lend you the two thousand.||It is not about you and it is not about whether I think you would pay it back. I made a rule some years ago about not lending money to people I want to stay friends with, after it went badly twice, and I have kept to it since.||What I can do: I can give you three hundred, as a gift, now, with nothing attached and no expectation of it coming back. And I am happy to sit down with the numbers with you this week if a second pair of eyes would help.||I know that is not what you asked for and I am sorry.||Ade

**What is doing the work.** A stated rule, not a judgement about this person. A rule is impersonal and cannot be argued with, and it lets the refusal avoid saying the unsayable thing about trust. The smaller gift is what stops the letter being a pure no. It is framed as a gift on purpose, so that it cannot become a second debt.

---

> "Your friends are planning something you cannot afford." — Saying you cannot afford it

**The letter.** All,||The villa looks wonderful and I am going to have to sit this one out. It is more than I can spend this year.||I am telling you now rather than being vague about dates until it goes away, which is what I would normally do.||Please do book it without me, genuinely, and please do not adjust it to a cheaper thing on my account, because then I will feel worse about it than I do now.||Can we do something at mine in September instead? I will cook and it will cost nobody anything.||Ade

**What is doing the work.** Saying the actual reason. The alternative, which is what most people do, is a fog of scheduling difficulties that everyone half-believes and that has to be maintained for months. Forbidding them to downgrade the trip is what prevents the second social cost, which is being the person who spoiled it, and the counter-offer keeps you in the group.

---

> "You need to ask your parents for help." — Asking family for money

**The letter.** Mum, Dad,||I need to ask you for some help and I have been dreading writing this, which is why it is a letter.||The position is this: I am eleven hundred short on the deposit and the offer is conditional on completing by the eighth. I have exhausted the other options, including the ones you would ask me about.||What I am asking for is a loan of eleven hundred, which I would pay back at two hundred a month starting in April. I would like it to be a loan and I would like us to write down the terms, because I do not want it to become a thing that is never mentioned and always there.||If the answer is no, that is genuinely all right and I will not ask again.||I love you both.||Murtaza

**What is doing the work.** Insisting on terms. Family money curdles because it is given without paperwork and then carries an unspoken interest rate paid in obligation for a decade. Asking for it to be documented is what protects the relationship, and it also signals seriousness in a way that no amount of gratitude does. Pre-empting *have you tried* saves a round of correspondence.

---

> "Somebody always pays less than their share and nobody says anything." — The unequal arrangement

**The letter.** Priya,||Slightly uncomfortable subject, and I would rather raise it than get quietly annoyed about it, which is where I am heading.||With the house bills, I have been covering the internet and the standing charges as well as my half of everything else, and it has drifted into being the arrangement rather than a favour. It is about sixty a month.||I do not think this was deliberate at all. It is the sort of thing that just happens.||Could we redo the split from March, and I am happy to do the spreadsheet? And if there is a reason the current arrangement suits you that I do not know about, tell me and we will work something out.||Ade

**What is doing the work.** Giving the other person an innocent explanation and an exit. *I do not think this was deliberate* is offered before any defence is needed, which prevents the conversation becoming about character. The offer to do the administrative work is what makes the change happen, and the closing question leaves room for a real reason to exist.
