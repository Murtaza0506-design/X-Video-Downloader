# "Project management" is five or six different jobs

Written before seeing the CV, so treat it as a map of the territory rather
than a recommendation. The point of it is that "I want to go into project
management" is not yet a target — these roles are screened differently,
pay differently, and reward different backgrounds. Picking one changes
every application the search produces.

## The role families

**Project Manager (delivery).** Owns scope, schedule, budget and risk for a
defined piece of work with an end date. Screened on the *size* of what
you've run: budget, headcount, duration, and sector. Sector matters more
here than anywhere else — construction PM and IT PM are close to
non-transferable in the eyes of a screener.

**Programme Manager.** Several related projects plus the benefits they are
meant to deliver. Screened on seniority of stakeholders handled and on
whether you've owned outcomes rather than plans. Rarely a first PM job.

**PMO (analyst → manager → head of).** Governance, reporting, standards,
assurance, portfolio data. The most common *entry route* for people without
a prior PM title, because it rewards organisation, reporting and tooling
rather than a track record of delivery. Often undervalued by candidates and
genuinely a fast route to PM.

**Delivery Manager (agile / digital).** Team-level: flow, impediments,
dependencies, team health. Dominant in tech companies and, in the UK, in
government digital. Screened on agile fluency and on whether you talk about
teams rather than Gantt charts. Little interest in PRINCE2 here.

**Scrum Master / Agile Coach.** Process and coaching for one or more teams.
Certification (PSM/CSM) is close to a hard filter; experience of actually
changing how a team works is what separates the shortlist.

**Technical Program Manager (TPM).** Cross-team technical dependencies,
usually at larger tech firms. Expects genuine technical literacy — you will
be asked to reason about systems, not just schedules. Pays the best of this
list and screens the hardest.

**Adjacent, frequently confused:** *Product Manager* is a different job —
it owns what gets built and why, not when and how. People pivot between
them, but a CV aimed at both lands at neither. *Change Manager*
(adoption, comms, training; Prosci is the common credential) and
*Implementation / Onboarding Manager* (customer-facing delivery in SaaS)
are both real landing spots for people with strong stakeholder skills and
no delivery track record.

## What a PM CV is screened on

In rough order of how much weight a screener puts on it:

1. **Scale, in numbers.** Budget, headcount, duration, users affected,
   volumes. A PM CV without numbers reads as junior regardless of the years
   on it. This is the single highest-leverage fix on most CVs.
2. **Sector match.** Fair or not, this is a heavy filter.
3. **Methodology, named.** Waterfall, agile, hybrid — and which flavour.
4. **Certifications.** PRINCE2 and APM (PMQ/PFQ) in the UK; PMP via PMI in
   the US and internationally; PSM/CSM for scrum; SAFe for scaled agile;
   MSP for programmes, P3O for PMO. Cheap signalling relative to their cost,
   and some postings filter on them outright.
5. **Tooling.** Jira, Confluence, MS Project, Smartsheet, Asana, Monday,
   Planview, Clarity. Name the ones you've actually used.

## If there is no prior PM title on the CV

The routes that actually work, in rough order of odds:

- **Internal pivot** — move into delivery at an employer that already trusts
  you. Highest success rate by a wide margin, and worth exhausting before
  the open market.
- **PMO analyst / project coordinator** — the standard side door. Lower
  title, real PM experience, promotes quickly.
- **Domain-specialist PM** — a PM role in the sector you already know, where
  your domain knowledge covers for the missing PM track record.
- **Delivery-adjacent titles** — implementation manager, service delivery,
  operations manager, business analyst.

Which of these is right depends entirely on what the CV already contains,
which is why the CV comes first.
